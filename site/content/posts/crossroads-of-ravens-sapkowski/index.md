I've finished reading `Rozdroże kruków` by Andrzej Sapkowski today. My Polish is still mostly in read-only mode, so I'd rather summarise in English.

## Plot

Geralt just starts his professional journey, finds a friend/tutor, has a bunch of adventures (each described in detail), makes a bunch of mistakes due to being not experienced enough (thus less cynical than in earlier books). 
I can't recall any erotic scene, unlike in the earlier books. There were dozens of explicit scenes though, where explicit stands for violence. And the whole story feels less epic, but other than that it's the very same style and values I saw in the whole series.

## Timeline

I started reading the series around 2014, and finished around 2015. It was about five books, each one being more epic than the previous one. Then a geeky notation of "The Witcher 0.5", "The Witcher 0.7" was introduced a few years later, and I guess I read that as well.

The current book - "Crossroads of Ravens" - is "The Witcher 0.1".

## Language

This was the first book of Sapkowski which I read in Polish 🤗. While 90+% of understanding was enough in order to follow the plot, it wasn't enough to enjoy it.
So I created a chat in the ChatGPT mobile app, which was auto-named `Nauka słówek z Sapkowskim`, where I enter a word in Polish and get the response in Polish as well, with all the nuances, as well as a simple explanation.

It was funny that I decided to do that only when about half of the book was done, in order to check if Oxenfurt is a translation of Oxford, or is it an allusion. Learned what "ford" means in English 😀:

> The name Oxford is of Old English origin ... meaning "ford of the oxen". It refers to a shallow river crossing, specifically on the Thames, where oxen or cattle could safely cross.

> The name Stanford is of Old English origin, meaning "stony ford" or "rocky river crossing." It is derived from the words stan (stone) and ford (river crossing)
